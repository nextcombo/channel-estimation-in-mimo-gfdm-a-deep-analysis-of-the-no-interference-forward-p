# Channel Estimation in MIMO-GFDM: A Deep Analysis of the No Interference Forward Pilot Injection (IFPI) Technique with MATLAB Code

✿Undisturbed Pilot Injection Technique for MIMO-GFDM Channel Estimation ✿Abstract Generalized Frequency Division Multiplexing (GFDM) is a flexible non-orthogonal waveform, its flexibility makes it a framework for simulating various multicarrier waveforms, including Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) and Single Carrier Frequency Domain Equalization (SC-FDE). However, according to the choice of filters, there may be inter-symbol interference and inter-carrier interference in GFDM; under the MIMO scenario, antenna inter-interference further challenges receiver design. This paper focuses on pilot-aided channel estimation in GFDM, different from previous research, proposes a pilot insertion technique that makes the pilot symbols orthogonal to the data symbols in the frequency domain. Based on this design, the frequency domain channel estimation algorithm originally developed for OFDM can be directly applied. This paper also analyzes the impact of this pilot design on signal characteristics (including Power Spectral Density (PSD) and Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR)). Finally, the performance of the MIMO-GFDM system is evaluated through simulations and compared with traditional MIMO-OFDM systems.  
Introduction Modern communication systems demand more than just throughput and data capacity. Low latency applications in the Internet of Things, dynamic spectrum access in cognitive radio, and coarse synchronization and bursty traffic in smart cities, among other demands, motivate both academic and industrial research into non-orthogonal waveforms. In broadband communication, due to the frequency selectivity of the channel, receivers need complex pilot insertion and channel estimation algorithms to accurately estimate and equalize the effects of the channel.  
Non-orthogonal multicarrier waveforms generate self-interference, causing the reference signal (i.e., pilot) to be affected by data symbols. Compared to the ability to achieve undisturbed observation of pilots in Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), such data symbol interference to the pilot symbol will reduce the performance of channel estimation, thus making the channel estimation algorithms based on OFDM (such as related literature [3][4][5] etc.) unable to be directly applied to non-orthogonal waveforms.
Generalized Frequency Division Multiplexing (GFDM) is a widely studied non-orthogonal waveform. Studies related to it (such as literature [6][7][8]) indicate that MIMO-GFDM systems, by leveraging frequency diversity, are expected to outperform MIMO-OFDM systems in terms of performance. This paper applies the proposed pilot design to the MIMO-GFDM system. In previous research on GFDM system channel estimation (literature [9][10][11]), there was interference between data symbols and pilot symbols. Literature [12] adopted the idea of separating pilot symbols from data symbols, but its method required calculating the precoder based on known data and pilot information at the transmitter, and was only applicable to approximately flat fading channels.
The contribution of this paper lies in modifying the GFDM signal to implement orthogonal pilot insertion, thereby achieving uninterrupted channel estimation for GFDM, which is also applicable to other non-orthogonal multicarrier systems using frequency domain channel estimation. Based on a low-complexity GFDM transmitter, this paper aims to insert pilot subsymbols such that the received signal's frequency domain is not affected by the data subsymbols at the pilot points. At the receiver end, extracting the orthogonal pilot can achieve uninterrupted channel estimation performance. However, due to modifications to the GFDM structure, the characteristics of the transmitted signal may differ from those of the original GFDM signal. Therefore, this paper presents the corresponding characteristics of the modified GFDM and compares its receiver performance with that of the original GFDM and OFDM under frequency selective MIMO block fading channels.
The core method proposed in this paper is the Interference Free Pilot Insertion (IFPI) technique, which focuses on making the pilot symbols orthogonal to the data symbols in the frequency domain, thus eliminating the interference of data symbols on the pilots. The specific implementation process is as follows:
1. Guidance Transmission in GFDM modulation, reserved specific frequency points for guidance transmission to ensure it is not affected by data symbols. By modifying the expression of the GFDM signal, introducing a permutation matrix and scaling factor, where the purpose is to assign any frequency point within the subcarriers for guiding the transmission, and to normalize the guidance energy to 1. In this design, only the signals corresponding to the guidance subcarrier are contributed by the guidance term (data term zero), and only the signals corresponding to the data subcarrier are contributed by the data term (guidance term zero), avoiding information superposition. For example, when (unit matrix), the first (number of transmit antennas) sub-symbols of the guidance subcarrier are processed directly in the frequency domain, isolated from other sub-symbols (provided that the guidance is located at a frequency point without carrier interfering), each orthogonal sub-symbol can be assigned to a specific transmit antenna, allowing the (number of receive antennas) MIMO channel to be decomposed into individual SISO channels for processing.
2. Received Pilot Extraction: Since the pilots of each transmit antenna are orthogonal to other sub-symbols, it is possible to use existing estimation algorithms to separately estimate the channel between each transmit-receive antenna pair. A matrix is defined (containing only the rows corresponding to the transmit antenna's pilot points in the DFT matrix), and from the received signal at the receive antenna, the corresponding pilot signal of the transmit antenna can be extracted. The extracted pilot signal contains only the information of the transmit antenna's pilot in the frequency domain, unaffected by data sub-symbol interference. Subsequently, the channel can be estimated using either the Least Squares (LS) or Linear Minimum Mean Square Error (LMMSE) algorithm.
3. Complexity Analysis
Thanks to the orthogonality of pilots and data in the frequency domain, the channel estimation complexity of IFPI-GFDM is the same as OFDM, lower than traditional GFDM. Traditional GFDM needs to treat data interference as additional correlated noise, requires additional signal processing, and needs to process more frequency domain pilot samples. Its LMMSE channel estimation complexity is (number of pilot subcarriers); whereas the LMMSE channel estimation complexity of IFPI-GFDM and OFDM is (number of pilot subcarriers). Although the complexity of transmitter for IFPI-GFDM using frequency domain GFDM modulation is slightly higher than that of time domain modulation traditional GFDM, considering the overall complexity of channel estimation, its overall data transmission complexity is still lower than traditional GFDM.
Conclusion This paper proposes an uninterrupted pilot insertion technique for non-orthogonal multi-carrier system channel estimation, although applied to MIMO-GFDM as an application scenario, it is also applicable to other non-orthogonal waveforms using frequency domain channel estimation. The coding performance of different GFDM receivers was evaluated through simulations and compared with an OFDM system with imperfect channel knowledge.
Simulation results show that IFPI-GFDM achieves the same channel estimation performance as OFDM, significantly better than previous GFDM channel estimation algorithms under high SNR (Reference [9][10]). In scenarios where channel knowledge is incomplete, the error rate (BER) performance of IFPI-GFDM is comparable to OFDM, while the traditional GFDM pilot insertion method suffers significant performance loss at high code rates.
Future research directions include applying this GFDM channel estimation algorithm to more challenging scenarios (such as high dual selective channels), and comparing the performance between GFDM and OFDM in terms of time selectivity - existing research (Reference [6]) indicates that GFDM outperforms OFDM in frequency selectivity.
